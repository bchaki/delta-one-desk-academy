## Learn

A Delta One desk sells and finances **linear exposure**: products whose value moves one-for-one with an underlying asset, so their delta is one and there is no optionality to price. Equity swaps, index futures, ETFs, cash equities, stock borrow and lend, repo and dividend products all live here. If a client wants the return of a stock or an index without the complexity of options, the Delta One desk is who they call. The product is simple. The business is not, because every trade creates financing, borrow, dividend, tax, margin, capital and operational consequences that have to be managed to the cent.

### What the desk actually sells

The desk sells three things, often bundled. **Exposure**: a client who wants to be long a stock, short a basket or tracking an index gets that through a swap, a future or a note. **Leverage and financing**: the client posts a fraction of the notional and the desk funds the rest, so a hedge fund can run a larger book than its capital would allow with cash stock. **Access**: markets that are hard for the client to trade directly because of local restrictions, tax or settlement. The desk is paid in **spreads** over a benchmark, in **commission and execution** revenue, in **borrow and lending** margins and in the carry it earns from its own funding advantage.

### Who sits where

A desk is a small team surrounded by larger functions. **Sales** owns the client relationship and quotes spreads. **Traders** hedge the resulting exposure and manage the risk book. **Structurers** build bespoke baskets and notes. The **TDA** (trading desk assistant, here an AVP) sits at the centre: books trades, runs the daily process, reconciles the numbers and chases breaks, so that every other role can rely on the book being right. Around them: **middle office** validates trades and PnL, **operations** settles cash and securities, **finance and product control** own the official PnL and balance sheet, **risk** sets and monitors limits, **funding and treasury** price internal funding, **legal and documentation** handle ISDAs and CSAs, **tax** handles withholding and 871(m), **compliance** watches conduct, and **technology** keeps the systems running. The lifecycle swimlane in this module shows who touches a trade and when.

### The client base

Clients are mostly **hedge funds** (leverage and shorting, the main swap users), **asset managers and pension funds** (index exposure, transition management, access), **insurers** and **corporates** (hedging, structured notes) and **internal desks** (equity finance, structured products, prime brokerage). Each client has a legal entity, a documentation set, a margin method and a credit limit. A swap is only as good as the paper behind it, which is why the booking checks in this academy look at the ISDA reference and the CSA before anything else.

### How the desk makes money

Revenue comes from a small number of repeating sources. **Spread on swaps**: the client pays benchmark plus spread, the desk funds the hedge at benchmark plus its own funding cost, and the difference, multiplied by notional and time, is the edge. **Execution and commission** on cash and futures flow. **Securities lending**: borrowing from long-term holders and on-lending to those who want to short, keeping a share of the fee. **Financing and repo**: earning more on collateral than the funding costs. **Internalisation**: matching client longs and shorts in-house so the desk saves external borrow and funding costs and keeps the saving. **Dividend and tax positions**: pass-through and withholding differences. Against revenue sit **costs**: balance sheet usage, capital, counterparty and funding valuation adjustments, trading costs, technology and people. A desk that quotes tight spreads and ignores these costs wins the flow and loses money.

### Risk that remains

Delta One is described as low risk, which is true of price direction (the desk hedges it) and false of everything else. The residual risks are **basis** (the hedge does not track the client exposure exactly), **dividend** (forecast versus pass-through), **borrow and recall** (the cost of shorts and the risk of losing them), **funding** (the spread to the benchmark moves), **operational** (booking and process errors, which are the most common source of unexplained PnL) and **counterparty** (the client fails). The academy teaches each of these in turn and then shows how they combine in the PnL.

### Why linear products are not trivial

A swap's PnL on a normal day is the hedge cancelling the client's equity move and the desk keeping a small spread. The PnL a TDA is asked to explain is the leftover: a dividend that was not passed through, a fixing off by 10 bps, a stale price on a thinly traded name. The work is a discipline of reconciliation, not of prediction. The people who do it well know the economics of every cash flow and can tell, from the size and sign of an unexplained number, which kind of break it is.

## Worked example

Consider one long swap from sale to settlement and see who does what.

**Step 1: the client call.** A hedge fund's PM asks sales for a 25 million long swap on a single stock. Sales asks the trader for the cost: funding for a long, balance sheet, capital and a margin. They quote 45 bps over the benchmark.

**Step 2: booking.** The TDA books the swap from the trade ticket, checks the ISDA reference, the CSA status, the legal entity, the spread, the benchmark and whether the underlying needs the 871(m) flag. A wrong entity here would trade under the wrong paper.

**Step 3: the hedge.** The trader buys the stock. The hedge is booked and linked to the swap. From now on the equity moves cancel.

**Step 4: the daily process.** Each day the TDA checks prices and fixings, reviews the PnL explain, and looks at anything that does not reconcile. Funding accrues. Dividends are tracked from the ex-date.

**Step 5: reset.** On the reset date the TDA confirms the fixing and reset price, releases the cash flows and sends the client statement.

**Step 6: break.** Suppose the booked spread was 35 bps instead of 45. The desk is under-earning 10 bps on 25 million, about 6,900 a month. The PnL explain shows it as an unexplained residual. Finding the cause takes a few minutes if you know the economics and days if you do not.

**Step 7: unwind.** At the end the client closes the trade and the hedge is sold the same day. Final accruals are paid and the trade's lifecycle closes.

## Desk reality

**Your day is shaped by the cut-offs.** Resets, trade bookings, margin calls and settlement all have deadlines. Learn them first, and learn who to call when you are about to miss one.

**Trust is the asset.** Traders and sales rely on you for the numbers. A TDA who flags a problem early, with the evidence attached and a proposed fix, builds a reputation faster than one who is right but late.

**Stay on the conduct side of every line.** Information about client flow is confidential and market-sensitive. Share it only with those who need it, and never trade on it for yourself. The conduct module covers this explicitly.

**Ask what a number means before you chase it.** The same word, such as spread, can mean a client spread over a benchmark or a hedge cost, and mixing them up is the cause of many avoidable breaks.
